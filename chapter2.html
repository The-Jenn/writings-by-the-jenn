Chapter 2: Temptation

The annual cul de sac block party was exactly the kind of forced socialization Lisa usually tried to endure with as much grace as possible. The street was barricaded off with orange cones, and neighborhood kids were yelling as they ran through a sprinkler set up on a lawn. Betsy was sitting on a porch steps a few houses down, with friends looking suitably mortified by the entire event.

Lisa sat in a folding camping chair near the edge of her own driveway. She had her dark aviator sunglasses on, shielding her eyes from the sun and hiding exactly where she was looking.

She was already on her third beer.

Across the street, Becky was in her element. She was holding a plate of deviled eggs and deep in conversation with Mrs. Gallacher.

Carla strolled out of her front door, and the entire energy of the block party shifted. She wasn’t wearing anything overly scandalous, but somehow, she managed to turn heads. She wore a short olivegreen dress that ended just above her knees. It looked incredibly comfortable, made of a thin material that clung perfectly to her thighs and the curve of her hips. Her hair was down, falling in loose, natural waves, and even from twenty feet away, Lisa could catch the faint scent of that perfume cutting through the smell of barbecue smoke.

Lisa gripped her cold beer can tighter, her jaw clenching.

It didn’t take long for the neighborhood to react. Within seconds, Greg from down the street was offering her a fresh plate from the grill, and two other husbands were suddenly very interested in the landscaping near where Carla was standing. They tripped over their words, laughing too loudly at whatever she said.

The wives were divided. A few younger women nervously approached her, while most of the older women whispered quietly.

Through it all, Lisa just watched. The dark lenses of her sunglasses were hiding her eyes. She let her gaze travel down the smooth line of Carla's neck, lingering on the delicate gold chain resting against her collarbone, before dropping to the slit in the side of the dress.

Lisa took a long sip of her beer. She was starting to feel it loosen her body a bit.

After politely declining a hot dog and dodging a boring conversation about the neighborhood, Carla finally removed herself from the crowd. She scanned the driveway, her eyes landing on Lisa sitting alone in the shade. A slow smile touched her lips, and she started walking over.

Out of the corner of her eye, Lisa saw Becky stop talking. Becky was watching them from across the street.

"Detective," Carla greeted, stopping right in front of Lisa's chair. She didn't sit. She just stood there, letting the afternoon sun catch the glow of her tanned skin.

"Carla," Lisa replied, keeping her voice carefully neutral. She didn't take her sunglasses off. "You decided to brave the local wildlife."

"I figured I should make an appearance. Mingle with the locals." Carla glanced over her shoulder at the group of husbands who were still watching her. "They're very... eager."

"They don't get out much," Lisa said dryly.

Carla laughed, a genuine sweet sound that made Lisa’s stomach tighten. Carla shifted her weight, the fabric of her dress touching Lisa's knee. "And what about you? Hiding behind the sunglasses in the shade. Are you on duty, or just observing?"

"Observing," Lisa said. She took another sip of her beer, feeling unexpectedly bold. "You were putting on quite a show."

Carla looked down at her. She stepped a fraction of an inch closer, lowering her voice so that it barely carried over the noise of the kids playing in the sprinkler.

"I'm just wearing a dress, Lisa. If people want to look, let them look." Carla paused, tilting her head slightly. "Though I have to admit, I prefer it when the audience is a little more appreciative. Some people stare, but they don't really see anything."

Carla knew exactly what Lisa had been doing as soon as she walked out and it was watching her.

Lisa should have shut the conversation down, stood up, and walked over to her wife. But the beer had loosened the tight knot in her chest, and the smell of Carla's perfume was scrambling her brain.

Instead of backing away, Lisa tilted her head back, looking directly up at Carla through the dark lenses.

"I see plenty," Lisa murmured, her voice dropping lower.

Carlas heart skipped, just slightly. It was a tiny break in her confident self, and it sent a rush of pure adrenaline straight through Lisa’s veins. Carla’s eyes dropped briefly to Lisa’s lips, a heavy, simmering heat.

"Lisa?"

The sharp voice broke the spell between them.

Lisa snapped her head up. Becky was walking across the asphalt toward them, an empty plastic plate in her hand and a forced smile on her face. Her eyes darted suspiciously between Lisa and Carla.

"Everything okay over here?" Becky asked, stopping next to Lisa's chair.

Carla didn't miss a beat. The heavy, erotic tension vanished in a second, replaced immediately by the polite, slightly bored neighbor attitude.

"Perfectly fine," Carla said smoothly, taking a step back. "Lisa was just giving me some tips on the best places to get takeout around here. I'm still trying to find my way around."

"Oh," Becky said, clearly thrown off by the boring topic. "Well, there's a great Italian place on Bleeker Street. We go there for anniversaries."

"Sounds lovely," Carla said. She offered Becky a polite fake smile, then let her gaze flick back to Lisa one last time. "I should get going. I have a deadline to meet. Enjoy the rest of your beer, Detective."

Lisa didn't say a word. She just watched Carla walk away, her hips swaying slightly as she walked her way through the lawn chairs and back to her house.

"She is so strange," Becky muttered, crossing her arms as she watched Carla disappear inside. "What were you two actually talking about?"

"Nothing," Lisa lied easily, lifting her beer to her lips to hide the smirk she couldn't quite suppress.

——-

Three weeks had passed since the block party, and the heat of summer had settled over the neighborhood.

For Lisa, those three weeks had been boring and mundane. She had kept her head down, worked double shifts at the precinct, and played the part of the attentive wife. But every time she heard a car door slam next door, the knot in her chest pulled a little tighter.

Now, the house was empty.

Becky and Betsy had left on Monday for a week long school trip to Washington, D.C. in the states. It was a huge trip Betsy had begged her moms to let her go on. I was expensive but it was a good opportunity. Lisa had stayed behind to testify in a string of court cases, but her schedule had finally cleared, leaving her with a four day weekend and a quiet house.

It was past midnight on Thursday. Lisa was sitting in the dark on her back patio, bourbon in her glass. It was her third bourbon. A soft, bluesy guitar track played quietly from the portable speaker on the table beside her. She had her head thrown back against the chair, her eyes closed, letting the alcohol dull her tense shoulders.

"I was wondering how long it would take you to come outside."

Lisa opened her eyes slowly, smiling and hoping she would come over.

Carla was standing at the warped section of the fence. She was wrapped in an oversized, heavy gray cardigan pulled tight over a simple black tank top and jeans and barefoot. Her hair was messy, and she was holding a half empty glass of red wine.

"Have you?" Lisa said, her voice a little rougher than usual thanks to the bourbon. "Just keeping a low profile."

Carla rested her forearms on the top of the fence. "I noticed the minivan has been gone since Monday. The neighborhood feels practically deserted without your wife patrolling the sidewalks." Carla said laughing at herself.

"School trip," Lisa murmured. She took a slow sip of her drink. "They're gone until Sunday."

"So, the detective is off the leash." Carla took a sip of her wine, her eyes studying Lisa. "No rules. No schedules. And you choose to spend it sitting in the dark, drinking alone."

Lisa let out a dry laugh. She pushed herself up from the chair and walked slowly over to the fence, stopping just on her side of the wood. The bourbon made her limbs feel heavy, but her mind was suddenly razor sharp.

"The quiet is nice," Lisa admitted, leaning her shoulder against the fence post. They were close enough now that Lisa could see the dark circles under Carla's eyes. She just looked tired.

"Is it?" Carla asked softly. She looked down at her wine glass, tracing the rim with her thumb. "I hate the quiet. It’s too loud in my head. That’s the problem with what I do. You spend all day making up fantasies, and then you look up and realize you're just sitting alone in a giant, empty house in the suburbs."

Lisa frowned slightly, surprised by the sudden vulnerability. "I thought you liked the escape. You told Becky you moved here for a change of pace."

"I told Becky what she wanted to hear," Carla said, a smile touching her lips. "I moved here because my publisher demanded a new manuscript in six months, and if I stayed in the city, I would have drank myself into a hospital before I wrote a single chapter. I needed isolation." She let out a long, slow breath. "Turns out, I'm terrible at being isolated."

Lisa watched her, the music filling the silence between them. For the first time, Lisa didn't see the reckless neighbor or the disruption to her life. She saw a woman under a lot of amount of pressure, hiding it behind loud music and late nights.

"I get it," Lisa said quietly.

Carla looked up, her eyes locking onto Lisa's. "Do you?"

"I spend my entire day enforcing rules," Lisa said, staring down at the ice melting in her bourbon. "I put bad people away. I bring order to chaos. And then I come home, and I have to be the perfect, stable wife. The reliable mother. I have to keep the yard looking nice. It's a different kind of performance, but it's exhausting."

Lisa paused, the alcohol loosening a truth she hadn't even admitted to herself. "And the worst part is, I'm sitting out here tonight, knowing my wife and daughter are thousands of miles away... and I don't miss them. I just feel relieved."

The admission hung in the air, heavy and deeply shameful. Lisa braced herself for the judgment, the sudden realization of how broken she sounded.

Instead, Carla reached across the top of the fence.

Her fingers gently brushed against the back of Lisa's hand where it gripped the glass. The touch was light, but it sent a shockwave straight through Lisa's chest.

"It's not a crime to want something for yourself, Lisa," Carla whispered, her voice incredibly soft. "You spend your whole life holding up the sky for everyone else. It makes sense that you'd want to drop it eventually."

Lisa stared at Carla's hand, then slowly looked up into her eyes. The physical attraction had been there since day one, undeniable. But this emotional understanding, was much more dangerous.

They stood there in the dark for a long time, the wooden fence the only thing keeping Lisa's world from completely caving in.

Lisa slowly moved back, wrapping her fingers tightly around her glass. The sudden absence of Carla's touch left her skin tingling. She cleared her throat, looking down at the patio stones.

Carla didn't push. She let her hand drop back to her side, a small smile touching her lips as she seamlessly shifted gears.

"Since you're flying solo this week," Carla said, her tone suddenly lighter. "Let me take you to dinner tomorrow. Show me the area. I haven't ventured out past that depressing local grocery store yet, and I could use a guide."

Lisa hesitated. Dinner. Just the two of them. It felt incredibly dangerous, but the word slipped out before she could stop it.

"I... yeah. Okay. Tomorrow."

"Seven," Carla said softly. She tapped the wood of the fence, turned, and walked back across her yard. Lisa stayed at her spot, watching until the glass door shut behind her.

Inside, Carla locked the door and walked straight through the dark, quiet house to her office. She dropped her wine glass on the desk and sank into her leather chair. Her laptop lit up.

She rested her chin in her hand, staring at the blinking cursor on the blank page, and smiled.

What exactly was she doing? She didn’t flirt with married women. She didn't really flirt with women at all. Sure, there had been a few drunken kisses at college parties, and she wasn't immune to noticing when a woman was attractive. But as far as her actual sex life went, she was straight. She had never been with a woman.

But Lisa Swain was different. There was something about the detective, the posture, the way she wore her authority, the quiet desperation hiding just behind her eyes, that was twisting something deep inside Carla's chest. It was a gravitational pull she had never felt before, and the fact that Lisa was so repressed only made Carla want to push her harder.

Carla pulled the keyboard closer. Her publisher had been breathing down her neck for the new manuscript. They wanted a fresh angle. A new dynamic.

Carla knew exactly what she was going to write.

A lesbian romance. It was completely uncharted territory for her, both personally and professionally. She might not have the physical experience yet, but her imagination was already running wild, fueled entirely by the woman sitting on the patio next door.

She rested her fingers on the keys, her smile widening and began to type.
