Chapter 26: Betrayal

Lisa stood at the kitchen island, cutting an apple. She sliced it into thin, even wedges, arranging them on a small plate next to a turkey sandwich.

It was one o'clock on a Saturday afternoon. The house was quiet.

For the first time in weeks, Lisa actually felt good. The panic that had been running her life had calmed a bit.

Knowing she had Carla, knowing they were in this together, something felt different.

She picked up the plate, turning toward the hallway to bring Becky her lunch.

As she walked down the hallway she heard crying. She stopped. She stood still, listening.

It was Becky.

"I just don't know what I'm doing wrong, Bets," Becky's voice drifted through the open bedroom door.

Lisa frowned, taking a step closer to the door. Betsy had gone in there ten minutes ago to drop off some clean laundry.

"Mom, don't cry," Betsy said. She sounded incredibly stressed, the tone of a teenager forced to parent an adult. "She's just tired. She's working and taking care of you."

"No, it's more than that," Becky cried. "You don't see how she looks at me when you aren't in the room. She looks at me like I'm a chore. Like I'm this huge burden she has to drag around. She doesn't touch me. She barely talks to me."

Lisa’s grip on the plate tightened.

"She's just stressed," Betsy pleaded, trying to rationalize it, Becky sounded uncomfortable.

"I think she wants to leave us," Becky whispered. "I really do, Betsy. I think she's just waiting for me to be able to walk again so she can pack her bags and walk out the door. She doesn't love me anymore. I'm broken, and she doesn't want a broken wife."

"Please don't say that," Betsy's voice cracked, upset now.

"I'm sorry, baby," Becky sobbed. "I shouldn't put this on you. You're just a kid. I just... I'm so scared. I'm stuck in this bed and I can feel her slipping away, and there is nothing I can do to stop it. If she leaves us, Betsy... what are we going to do?"

Lisa stood frozen in the hallway.

Becky wasn't just manipulating Lisa anymore. Because Lisa had stopped acting like a frantic, guilty mess, because Lisa was acting calm and composed, Becky had recognized the loss of control and immediately pivoted to the one thing Lisa loved more than anything in the world.

She was weaponizing their own daughter. She was intentionally terrifying a sixteen-year-old girl just to tighten the leash on Lisa.

The bedroom door suddenly opened.

Betsy walked out, holding an empty laundry basket against her hip.

Her face was flushed, with tears in her eyes. She nearly ran right into Lisa in the hallway.

Betsy stopped. She looked at the plate of food in Lisa’s hands, then looked up at Lisa's face. There was no anger in the teenager's eyes, only a deep sadness that made Lisa's chest ache.

"Betsy, hey," Lisa said softly, keeping her voice gentle.

Betsy just looked at her. Her chin trembled slightly. "Mom... are you going to leave us?"

The words hurt. Lisa felt a sharp ache in her throat. She wanted to pull her daughter into a hug and tell her the truth. She wanted to tell her that she thinks her mom didn't slip on those stairs, that she did it on purpose, that she was a monster holding them all hostage.

But she couldn't.

"No," Lisa said, stepping forward and setting the plate on edge of the small hallway table.

She reached out, resting her hands on Betsy's shoulders. "Look at me, Bets. I am not leaving you. I promise you, I am not going anywhere."

Betsy looked at the floor. "She thinks you are. And you just seem so far away lately. I don't know how to fix it."

"You don't have to fix anything," Lisa said, squeezing her shoulders. "It's just been a really hard month. We're all just tired."

Betsy gently pulled out of Lisa's grip. She set the empty laundry basket on the floor, wiping her cheeks with the sleeves of her shirt. "I just need some air."
