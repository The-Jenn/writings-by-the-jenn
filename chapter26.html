Lisa stared at her, frozen in shock.

"What?!" Lisa yelled back, the sheer audacity of the lie pushing her over the edge. "She was just standing at the window! She shoved me!"

"Stop fighting moms!" Betsy yelled, her voice breaking into a sob. She covered her ears with her hands, looking frantically between the two of them. "I can't take it! Just stop!"

Betsy reached down and grabbed Becky under the arms, trying to pull her up from the floor. Betsy strained, but she was too weak to lift dead weight, and Becky was refusing to help herself at all.

"Move," Lisa said firmly.

Lisa stepped in, shoving Betsy gently out of the way. She bent down, grabbed Becky under the arms, and picked her up.

Becky screamed and thrashed, but Lisa ignored it completely.

She half carried, half dragged Becky out of the kitchen and put her onto the living room couch.

Lisa stood over her, breathing heavily, her fists clenched at her sides.

"What the fuck, Becky?" Lisa asked. "What has gotten into you?"

Becky stopped crying instantly. She looked up at Lisa, her eyes dry and filled with rage.

"Why don't you tell me, Lisa?!" Becky yelled back.

Betsy backed up against the front door. She was crying hard, her hands over her mouth.

She didn't know what was happening. Her house had turned into a war zone again in a matter of seconds, and the way her mothers were looking at each other terrified her.

"Why don't you tell us, Lisa? Huh?" Becky challenged, sitting up on the couch, “Tell us where you were all night."

Lisa looked at Becky. She looked at the madness in her wife's eyes, the total willingness to destroy everything in the room just to maintain control.

Then Lisa looked back at Betsy, who was shrinking against the door, completely traumatized by the screaming.

Lisa couldn't do it. She couldn't stand in this living room and scream back. She couldn't win this fight right now without breaking her daughter.

Lisa turned her back on Becky. She walked straight toward Betsy.

"Mom, please," Betsy cried.

Lisa didn't stop. She reached past Betsy, grabbed the handle of the front door, and pulled it open. She walked out, pulled the door shut behind her, and walked to her car.

She didn't look back at the house. She got into the driver's seat, started the engine, and drove away, having absolutely no idea where she was going, only knowing she had to get out of that house before she did something she couldn't take back.

Inside the living room, the silence was painful after the front door slammed shut.

Betsy stood frozen by the door, staring at Her mom. "Mom... what is happening?" she asked, her voice shaking.

Becky sat on the couch, staring at the closed door. For a brief second, a little regret crossed her face.

She hadn't wanted Lisa to leave. That wasn't part of the plan.

Becky looked over at Betsy. She let her shoulders slump, her face softening back into the fragile, heartbroken mother.

"Oh, Betsy," Becky sighed. "I'm sorry. I'm so sorry you had to see that."

Betsy didn't move. She just wiped her eyes, looking at her mother with confusion.

"There are some things going on between me and your mom at the moment," Becky said quietly, looking down at her hands. "She's just... she's not there for me anymore. She's so angry all the time. I don't know how to fix it."

Becky looked up, her eyes wide and pleading. "Please, can you help me back to bed? I hurt so bad."

Betsy hesitated, but she walked over to the couch. She grabbed Becky by the arm and braced her shoulder.

Becky made a huge show of struggling to stand. She leaned her entire weight on Betsy, groaning loudly as they hobbled slowly down the hallway together.

They finally made it into the guest room. Betsy helped her down onto the mattress and pulled the blanket up over her legs.

"Thank you, sweetie," Becky whispered, closing her eyes.

Betsy didn't say you're welcome. She backed out of the room and closed the door. She stood alone in the hallway, looking toward the kitchen where the broken plates were still scattered across the floor. She knew something really bad was about to happen. She just didn't know what to do.

Lisa drove. She didn't have a destination in mind. She just needed to put distance between herself and the madness she had just walked away from.

Carla flashed into her mind. The warmth of her bedroom. The safety. Then the image of Becky screaming on the kitchen floor violently replaced it. Then work. The precinct, the paperwork, the normalcy she was desperately craving.

Lisa spotted an empty parking lot outside a closed strip mall and pulled the car in.

She threw the car into park, leaving the engine running, and grabbed her phone. She found Carla’s name and called.

Carla picked up on the first ring. "Lisa? Are you okay?"

She knew Lisa had left her house not that long ago. For Lisa to be calling already meant something had gone wrong.

Lisa leaned her forehead against the steering wheel, squeezing her eyes shut. "I just got into a massive fight with her. She was standing at the kitchen window when I walked in, Carla. Perfectly fine. Just standing there. And when I caught her, she threw a plate into the sink, started screaming at me, and then threw herself on the floor the second Betsy came downstairs."

Carla let out a frustrated sigh.

"She tried to tell Betsy I pushed her, or abandoned her, or I don't even know," Lisa continued. "I just left. I walked out the door and got in my car. Carla, it's time. I have to start making the move to get out of this house. I need to talk to Betsy alone, explain things, and then I am leaving Becky."

Carla just listened. She didn't interrupt, and she didn't try to calm Lisa down with empty promises. Carla was ready. She was in love with this woman, and she wanted her out of that trap badly. She just wasn’t sure Lisa would ever actually do it.

"Can I do anything, Lisa?" Carla asked.

"You've done enough, Carla," Lisa said. "I'm so sorry you got pulled into all of this. I..."

"Shhhh, hey," Carla interrupted quickly, cutting off the apology. "Meeting you has been the best thing in my life. I am not sorry I am in the middle of this. I only wish it were under different circumstances."

Lisa let out small laugh, "Yeah. Me too."

"Are you safe right now? Where are you?" Carla asked.

"I'm just sitting in a parking lot a few miles away," Lisa said. "I got in my car and drove off. I didn't know what to do after she made that scene in front of Betsy."

"How about you go get a hotel room for the night," Carla suggested. "Let her cool off. You need a break, and going back right now is just going to start another screaming match."

The suggestion was loaded, and they both knew it. A hotel room meant Carla could come to her, and they could actually be together without Lisa rushing off.

Lisa smiled, the tension easing just a little.

"I do need to go grab some stuff from the house first, though. Clothes. My toothbrush."

"Lisa, going back there right now isn't a good idea," Carla warned. "She's unstable. Just go to a store, buy what you need for tonight."

"I have to check on Betsy," Lisa insisted, her stubborn streak taking over. "I can't just leave her alone with Becky after that. I'll be quick. I'll just grab a bag and get out."

Carla sighed, knowing she couldn't win this argument. "Okay. Keep your phone on. Call me the second you leave."

Lisa hung up. She put the car in drive, turned out of the parking lot, and headed back toward the house.

When Lisa walked through the front door, the house was quiet. She walked cautiously toward the kitchen, half expecting Becky to be waiting for her, but the room was empty. The shattered plate and the coffee mug was still scattered across the floor and the counter.

Lisa walked to the utility closet, grabbed the broom and dustpan, and started cleaning up the mess.

She swept the broken pieces into a pile, dumped them into the trash can, and wiped down the counter.

Footsteps sounded on the staircase.

Lisa turned as Betsy walked into the kitchen. The teenager's face was pale, and her eyes were red and swollen.

"Mom..." Betsy said, her voice cracking instantly. She started crying again, crossing the kitchen quickly.

Lisa dropped the rag on the counter and caught her daughter, wrapping both arms securely around her.

Betsy buried her face in Lisa’s shoulder, holding on tight.

Lisa hugged her back, resting her chin on Betsy’s head, tears falling.

"Mom, please, will you tell me what's happening?" Betsy cried against her shirt.

"Oh, angel," Lisa whispered, smoothing down the back of Betsy’s hair. "Your mother and I just aren't seeing eye to eye at the moment. Things are just really tense right now."

Lisa hugged her tighter, hating the vague answer but knowing she couldn't drop the truth on a 16 year old while Becky was sleeping fifty feet away.

"Listen," Lisa said, pulling back just enough to look Betsy in the eye. "I've got the kitchen cleaned up. I am going to head to work for the night. I'm taking an overnight shift. They really need me on a case."

Lisa felt horrible lying to her face. She hated it. But she needed to get out of the house for the night before Becky completely messed with Betsy’s head again, and a work excuse was the only thing Becky couldn't argue with.

"It's okay, Mom, I understand," Betsy said, wiping her nose with the back of her hand. "I can take care of her for the night."

Betsy stepped back, turning toward the stairs.

"If anything happens, please call my cell, okay?" Lisa called out, watching her go. "I don't care what time it is. I'll get home as fast as I can."

Betsy managed a small, tired smile. "I will. Bye, Mom."

Lisa watched her walk upstairs. Once she heard Betsy’s bedroom door close, Lisa moved quickly.

She walked past the guest room, and went straight upstairs to her bedroom.

The room felt horrible to her now. She walked into the closet, heading for the very back where they kept the luggage and travel gear.

She looked up at the top shelf, spotting the black duffel bag she used for trips.

Lisa reached up and grabbed the handle, giving it a hard pull.

The bag was stuck. As it came loose, it dragged a cardboard box along with it.

The box had been hidden underneath the luggage.

The box tipped over the edge of the shelf, the lid falling off.

Stacks of loose paper came down, hitting Lisa on the shoulders and scattering all over the closet floor.

"Damn it," Lisa cursed, frustrated.

She threw the duffel bag onto the floor and bent down to pick up the mess.

She started gathering the papers into a pile. She planned to just shove them back into the box, but as she grabbed a handful of pages, a logo caught her eye.

It was a bank logo. But it wasn't the bank they used for their joint checking or savings accounts.

Lisa frowned. She looked closer at the top sheet. It was a formal bank statement, addressed solely to Becky.

Lisa stood up slowly, holding the stack of papers. She walked out of the closet and dumped the entire pile onto the center of the bed.

She started thumbing through the pages, spreading them out across the quilt.

"What the..." Lisa whispered.

She picked up a statement from two months ago. The balance at the bottom of the page made lose her breath. It was a massive amount of money.

Lisa frantically grabbed more papers, organizing them by date, trying to understand what she was looking at.

Lisa had always let Becky handle the finances. Lisa worked the long shifts, brought in the paycheck, and Becky managed the bills, the savings, and the investments.

Becky was brilliant with numbers, she was organized , so Lisa had never questioned a single decision. She simply trusted her wife.

But as Lisa read the statements, a horrifying picture began to form.

There were records of personal loans taken out in both of their names. There were transfer receipts showing hundreds of dollars at a time moving from their joint account into this hidden account.

The dates on the oldest statements went back 10 years.

10 years.

Becky had been siphoning money from Lisa's paycheck a bit at a time, for a decade.

She had built a hidden nest egg, taking out loans under Lisa’s name to cover the deficits in their regular accounts, perfectly balancing the books so Lisa would never notice the missing funds.

Lisa’s eyes went wide as the math finally clicked into place.

Becky wasn't just manipulating her emotionally. She wasn't just faking to keep Lisa trapped.

She had been financially trapping her for their entire marriage. If Lisa ever tried to leave, she would walk away completely broke and saddled with debt she didn't even know she had, while Becky sat on a fortune.

"What the fuck," Lisa said again.

Her hands were shaking. The betrayal was so thoroughly calculated, it made her feel physically sick.

The woman downstairs wasn't just controlling, she was a predator.

Lisa moved with sudden purpose. She grabbed every piece of paper off the bed, making sure she didn't leave a single receipt behind.

She shoved all of them into the duffel bag. She grabbed a pair of jeans, a clean shirt, and her toiletries from the bathroom, throwing them on top of the financial records, and zipped the bag shut.

She threw the strap over her shoulder and walked out of the bedroom.

She stopped at Betsy’s door, pushing it open slightly. Betsy was sitting on her bed, her laptop open in front of her.

"Hey," Lisa said. "I'm heading out."

Betsy looked up. "Okay. What do I tell Mom when she wakes up?"

"Just let her know I'm working," Lisa said, walking over and kissing the top of Betsy’s head. "I'll be back tomorrow. Call me if you need me."

"I will," Betsy promised.

Lisa walked down the stairs and straight out the front door. She got into her car, threw the duffel bag onto the passenger seat, and started the car.

She picked up her phone and typed a quick text to Carla.

I got my stuff. Going to the Marriott. Meet me there.
