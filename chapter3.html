Chapter 3: Dinner Date

Carla sat back and stared at the screen. A laugh escaped her. She was definitely done for the night. This was going to require a lot more thought, and a lot more field research.

She threw back the last swallow of her wine and shut her laptop. Standing up, she walked over to the side window of her office, the one that looked out over the fence line.

Lisa was still sitting exactly where she had left her. She was leaning back in the patio chair, staring straight ahead into the dark yard, completely motionless.

"That girl has some demons," Carla whispered to herself.

She reached up, her fingers lightly brushing her own lips where she had imagined the detective’s mouth would be. Shaking her head, Carla turned off the office light and headed to bed. For the first time since she’d moved to the suburbs, she was actually looking forward to tomorrow.

The next evening, Lisa’s bedroom looked like a teenagers room with clothing.

She had changed three times. First, a button down shirt that felt too stiff, like she was going into court. Then, a dark blouse that felt entirely too dressy for a casual dinner.

She stood in front of the full length mirror holding up a third option when she suddenly stopped and let out a dry laugh.

What are you doing? she asked her reflection. Why are you stressing over this? It was just dinner with a neighbor. A very attractive, intoxicating neighbor, sure, but still just dinner. She tossed the clothes onto the bed and reached for something simple. She pulled on a pair of dark denim jeans, a soft black V-neck t-shirt, and a white blazer over it to dress it up just enough. She slipped her feet into a pair of black heels, ran a hand through her blonde hair, and forced herself to exhale.

Right on cue, a knock echoed from downstairs.

Lisa froze for a second. She took a deep, breath, walked down the stairs, and opened the front door.

Carla stood on the porch, and Lisa felt her face get hot for a second. She wore tight black jeans tucked into black high-heeled boots, paired with a longer, slouchy black V-neck sweater that hung off one shoulder, exposing her collarbone.

As Carla stepped slightly closer, the evening breeze caught her scent. Her usual perfume. It was absolutely intoxicating.

"Uh... hi. Please, come in," Lisa managed to say, stepping back to pull the door wider.

Carla walked inside. She stopped in the foyer and looked around. Lisa’s house was immaculate, vacuumed rugs, perfectly aligned picture frames, not a single piece of clutter in sight. It was Lisa’s need for order.

"You have a lovely home," Carla said softly. She kept her hands buried in the pockets, and Lisa noticed something she had never seen from Carla before, she looked almost shy. The confident edge was gone, replaced by a quiet, nervous person.

"Thank you," Lisa said, feeling a sudden, warm beat in her chest at the sight of Carla out of her element. She grabbed her small leather purse from the entryway table. "So, where to?"

Carla looked at her, and smiled. "You're the guide, yeah? Where do you suggest?"

"Well, there's a bunch of random restaurants and bars over on Bleecker Street," Lisa offered, stepping out onto the porch and locking the door behind them. "It's got a little bit of everything. We could head there and just see what stands out to you?"

"That sounds perfect," Carla said.

Lisa walked down the front steps, leading the way toward the street just as a set of headlights swept around the corner. A yellow taxi pulled up.

"I hope you don't mind," Lisa said, turning back to Carla as they reached the sidewalk. "I called a cab. I'm not big on drinking and driving, being a cop and all."

Carla laughed. "Makes perfect sense to me."

Lisa stepped forward and pulled the door of the cab open, holding it as Carla slid into the backseat. The scent of her cologne lingered as she brushed past. Lisa took another deep breath, climbed in right behind her, and pulled the door shut.

"Bleecker Street, please," Lisa told the driver, as the cab pulled away from the suburbs and headed toward the city.

The ride to Bleecker Street was quick. The awkwardness fell into a comfortable silence. When the cab dropped them off. They found a small, bar. They sat corner.

"Bourbon, neat," Lisa told the bartender when he approached.

"Dirty martini. Extra olives," Carla said. Lisa looking at her smile as she talked to bartender.

Once the drinks arrived, the silence broke and they looked across the table at each other.

"So," Lisa said. "Did you go to college? Was writing always the plan? And ending up in the suburbs?"

Carla smiled, swirling the olive in her glass. "I got a Literature degree, originally. I was going to write a Great Novel. I spent four years in a tiny dorm room in the states reading Hemingway and deciding I was entirely too cynical for the real world." She took a sip of her drink. "What about you? Let me guess. Class president. Straight A's. Probably captain of the debate team."

Lisa let out a genuine laugh, the sound surprising even herself. "Close. Softball captain. Criminology major. I liked rules. I liked knowing that if someone crossed a line, there was a consequence. The world felt very chaotic when I was growing up, so I think I just gravitated toward structure."

"Structure," Carla repeated softly, her eyes staring at Lisa's throat as she took another drink. "And does it still feel like that? Like you're holding it down?"

"Most days," Lisa admitted.

They finished their first round, the conversation countinuing easily. When they stepped back out onto the street, the alcohol had giving Lisa a warm buzz over her nerves.

"Hungry?" Lisa asked.

"Starved," Carla replied.

Lisa led them two blocks down to a sushi restaurant with bright paper lanterns. They were seated at a small corner table, their knees touching because of the small area.

Over spicy tuna and yellowtail, the conversation shifted.

"Tell me about the job," Carla said picking at her food with her chopsticks. "The real job. Not the paperwork."

Lisa paused. She rarely talked about work with Becky. Becky didn't like the ugly parts of the world she preferred to pretend they didn't exist. But looking into Carla's intense eyes, Lisa felt a sudden urge to be entirely honest.

"It's heavy," Lisa said quietly, resting her forearms on the table. "You see people on the worst days of their lives. You see what people are capable of doing to each other behind closed doors. Sometimes it’s domestic cases. Sometimes it’s... worse. You take a piece of it home with you every night, no matter how hard you try to leave it at the precinct."

Carla listened intently, her sushi forgotten. She didn't offer any story or tell Lisa how brave she was. She just watched her, taking in Lisa's words.

"That's why you sit in the dark on the patio," Carla murmured, almost to herself.

Lisa froze as she lifted her drink to her lips. Does she watch me too? Lisa thought.

"Yeah," Lisa breathed. "It's the only place it’s quiet enough to stop thinking."

Carla reached out, her fingertips just barely grazing the cuff of Lisa’s white blazer. It was a gentle touch, meant to comfort, but it sent a chill straight up Lisa’s arm.

After they split the check, Lisa insisting, Carla fighting her for it finally giving Up with a smile, they walked back out into the night.

”One more?" Carla asked, looking up at Lisa. The lights reflected in her eyes, making them look seductive. "I'm not ready to go back to the suburbs yet."

"Me neither," Lisa said quietly staring Carla.

They found a dive bar a few streets over. They sat at the bar. They were sitting shoulder to shoulder now.

They ordered another round, Lisa sticking to bourbon, Carla switching to a whiskey ginger. The drinks were stronger here, and as the hour stretched past midnight, the alcohol started to loosen Lisa’s restraint.

"Okay, my turn," Lisa said, turning on her stool to face Carla. Their knees bumped, and neither of them pulled away. "You asked about my job. I want to know about yours. How do you write it? Where do you even get the ideas?"

Carla looked down at her glass, a sudden, unexpected flush in her cheeks. It was the first time all night she looked genuinely guarded.

"It's... complicated," Carla said, picking at the cardboard coaster.

"You write about sex for a living," Lisa teased gently, leaning in closer. The scent of Carla getting to her. "It can't be that complicated."

"The act isn't complicated," Carla corrected, looking up. "The emotion is. Writing erotica isn't just about putting A and B together. It’s about the vulnerability. It's about taking two people, stripping away all their armor, and figuring out what happens when they entirely surrender to each other." She traced the rim of her glass, choosing her words carefully. "It's incredibly personal. Sometimes it feels like taking off my own skin and putting it on the page. That's why I don't like talking about it."

Lisa watched her, completely mesmerized. The party girl was a million miles away. This woman was an artist, deep, highly observant, and protective of her world.

"I understand," Lisa said softly. And she did. She understood the need to protect the vulnerable parts of yourself from a world that didn't always get it.

Carla turned her head, her gaze locking onto Lisa's. The air between them suddenly felt tense.

Carla wanted to say it. She wanted to lean across the bar and tell Lisa exactly what she had been thinking since she saw her for the first time. She wanted to tell Lisa that her heart had been acting a fool, that the closeness was driving her crazy.

But as Carla let her eyes drop to Lisa's hand resting on the bar, the light caught the gold band on Lisa's left ring finger.

Tread lightly, Carla reminded herself. She had never been a home wrecker. She had never crossed this particular line. Lisa was married. Lisa had a kid. Lisa had a whole life that Carla had no business blowing up.

But God, she wanted to.

"You're staring," Lisa murmured, a slight, tipsy slur.

"I know," Carla whispered back, not looking away from Lisa's eyes. "I'm just trying to figure you out, Detective."

"Any luck?"

"Some," Carla said, a slow smile finally returning to her lips. "But I think you're a mystery that's going to take a lot to solve."

Lisa held her breath at that statement. She didn't pull back. She stared at Carla.

——-

The yellow cab pulled up to the quiet, dark curb of their suburban street. Lisa stepped out first, her heels clicking softly on the pavement, and turned to hold the door open. She offered a hand, and Carla took it, stepping out onto the sidewalk.

Lisa leaned back into the window to hand the driver a few bills, and the cab slowly pulled away.

"Thank you for tonight," Carla said softly, breaking the quiet. She wrapped her arms around herself. "I needed something like this."

Lisa turned to face her, her hands slipping into the pockets of her jeans. "Like this?"

"Something real," Carla responded, her eyes searching Lisa's face. "Something stimulating and calm, but still fun."

Lisa let out a soft laugh, the bourbon still warming her blood. "Not your usual all night partying?"

Carla tilted her head, a smile on her lips. "How do you know I'm out all night partying? "

Lisa completely froze. Her heart skipped a beat, sudden panic cutting through the alcohol buzz. She opened her mouth, scrambling for an excuse, terrified that she had just exposed how often she stood in the dark spare room watching Carla’s driveway.

Carla watched the panic flash in the detective's eyes, and her smile softened into something deeply amused. She felt like she knew exactly what Lisa was doing behind those drawn blinds.

"I'm kidding, Lisa," Carla murmured, though the low tone of her voice suggested she wasn't kidding at all.

They stood in silence for a long moment. The space between them felt incredibly small.

"I would invite you in for a drink, but..." Lisa stuttered, her gaze dropping to Carla's lips before snapping back up to her eyes.

"Oh, yeah, I totally get it. It's late," Carla said quickly, offering an easy out, even though the thought of going inside Lisa’s empty, quiet house was making her head spin.

Lisa looked at her again, the conflict openly on her face. She took a half-step back toward her own walkway. "G'night, Carla."

"G'night, Lisa," Carla whispered back, her voice barely carrying over the space between them.

Carla turned and started walking toward her porch. With every step she took, she could feel Lisa's eyes tracking her. It made her skin prickle. She smiled to herself, a thrill racing down her spine as she walked the sidewalk and climbed the stairs to her front door.

As she slid her key into the lock, she turned her head, looking back over her shoulder.

Lisa was still standing on the curb. She hadn't moved an inch, her white blazer glowing faintly in the dark, just watching her.

Carla stopped. She met Lisa's gaze across the lawns, holding it for one charged, breathless second. Then, she pushed the door open, stepped inside, and shut behind her.

The moment the door shut, Carla let her head fall back against the door. She closed her eyes, her heart beating a million miles a minute.

"What the fuck are you doing, Carla?" she whispered to the empty entryway.

She pushed off the door and walked straight down the hallway to her office. She didn't bother turning on the overhead lights, just clicked on the small brass desk lamp and flipped open her laptop. Her hands were actually trembling slightly as they hovered over the keys. She needed an outlet for the feelings she was having.

She opened the document she had started the night before, read the last line, and immediately began to type.
