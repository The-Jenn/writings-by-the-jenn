Sunday. Lisa had spent the night regretting her thoughts. She woke early and wanted to try and keep her mind off her thoughts. She needed to keep her hands busy so she backed her green Jeep Gladiator out of the garage, grabbed a bucket, and hooked up the hose.

Manual labor. It was the only way she was going to survive the next two hours before Becky and Betsy got home.

She wore a faded gray tank top and an old pair of cutoff shorts. She was scrubbing harder than necessary, letting the burn in her shoulders distract her. Water sprayed everywhere, soaking her sneakers and her denim shorts and her shirt.

Next door, the screen door opened.

Carla stepped out onto her porch holding a tall glass of iced tea and a black notebook. She had thrown on an oversized white button down, the sleeves rolled up, the collar open, and short shorts intending to sit in the shade and work through a chapter outline.

But as she reached her chair, she stopped.

Lisa was stretched up on her toes, leaning across the massive hood of the truck to reach the middle of the windshield. The awkward angle pulled her tank top tight, showing off the lean, solid muscle in her back and shoulders.

Carla stood perfectly still and watched her.

Lisa all wet like that made her grip her glass a little tighter. Lisa was rinsing the hood. The water ricocheted off the metal and splashed right back at her, soaking the front of her gray tank top and it instantly darkened and clung to her skin.

Carla swallowed hard. It wasn't just a fun, teasing game anymore. Watching the water drip down Lisa's legs, Carla had a real attraction and she wanted to do something about it.

Lisa dragged the back of her wrist across her forehead to push away a wet piece of hair. As she turned, she froze.

She saw Carla.

Lisa’s hands dropped. The hose kept running, water leaking around her shoes. Lisa wished she would just walk across this lawn and do something about it already.

Lisa’s chest started to rise and fall a little faster. She was dripping wet and completely turned on by Carla watching her.

Carla didn't look away, and she didn't offer a friendly wave. Instead, she slowly took a sip of her drink. Her eyes dropped to the soaked front of Lisa's shirt, lingered there for a long second, and then slowly dragged back up to meet Lisa's eyes. Carla took another sip, letting her teeth catch her bottom lip as she pulled the glass away.

Carla was letting her know. Lisa’s grip on the hose tightened. Lisa’s nipples got hard, her body reacting instantly. The urge to drop the hose, walk across the grass, and back Carla into the house was so strong it made her dizzy.

Then, she heard a car down the street.

Lisa jumped. She snapped her head toward the street just as Becky’s silver minivan rolled past the mailbox, slowing down to pull into the driveway.

When Lisa looked back to the porch, the screen door was just clicking shut. Carla was gone.

Lisa was left standing in the driveway, soaked to the bone and completely turned on, as her wife put the car in park and smiled at her through the windshield.

——-

Carla stood in the shadows of the entryway, peering through the mesh screen.

Out in the driveway, the minivan doors slid open. Betsy hopped out first, tossing a heavy duffel bag over her shoulder. She walked over to Lisa, who had just twisted the spigot to cut off the water, and gave her mom a quick, one armed hug.

Then Becky stepped out of the driver’s side. She looked rested, tan, and completely oblivious. Becky walked over to Lisa, a bright smile on her face as she looked her wife up and down.

"Well, look at you," Becky teased. "All wet and sexy. Did you miss me?"

Behind the screen door, Carla squeezed her eyes shut and turned away. Her chest ached. It was a jealousy that made her feel actually sick to her stomach. She leaned back against the wall of her hallway.

What the hell am I doing? she thought.

She was standing in the dark, spying on a married couple. She was twisting herself into knots over a woman who belonged to the cheerful, wholesome wife right next door. It was pathetic, and it was a line she had promised herself she would never cross. She needed to stop this before she did real damage. She needed to get her mind completely off Lisa Swain.

Carla pulled out her phone and started a quick group text. She needed loud music, she needed the city, and she needed a distraction.

A few hours later, Lisa was sitting at her dining room table. Betsy was picking at her food and rambling about the monuments in D.C., and Becky was talking about the hotel accommodations. Lisa nodded at all the right times, playing the part perfectly, but her eyes kept drifting past her family to the window that faced Carla’s driveway.

Right as Becky was asking Lisa how her quiet week had been, Carla’s front door opened.

Lisa’s fork paused halfway to her mouth. Carla stepped out onto her porch, and she was dressed like she wanted to pick up someone. She wore a skin tight, dark red dress that barely hit mid thigh, black strappy heels, and her hair was styled in thick, messy waves. She looked lethal. An Uber pulled up to the curb, and Carla slid into the back seat without a single glance toward Lisa's house.

Lisa swallowed hard, staring down at her plate.

At 5:00 AM the next morning, the house was dead quiet. Lisa was back in her element. Dark slacks, a crisp button down shirt, her holster and badge clipped securely to her belt. She stood in the dark kitchen, waiting for the coffee machine to finish brewing, trying to find comfort in the familiar, structured routine of her Monday morning.

Then she saw headlights.

Lisa walked over to the sink, looking out the blinds. A car was in Carla's driveway. The passenger door swung open, and Carla stumbled out. She was laughing, holding her heels in one hand.

The driver’s side door opened, and a man stepped out. He was tall, wearing a suit jacket. He walked around the front of the car, caught Carla by the waist, and pulled her flush against him. Carla giggled, leaning her head on his shoulder as he wrapped a heavy arm around her and guided her up the front steps. She fumbled with her keys, leaning into the man's chest, until the door finally opened and they disappeared inside.

Lisa stood frozen in the dark kitchen, the coffee pot beeping loudly behind her.

Reality check. Lisa gripped the edge of the granite counter. She needed to stop this. All of it. The lingering looks, the racing heart, the stupid, pathetic jealousy that was keeping her up at night. Carla was a chaotic party girl. She liked loud clubs, late nights, and clearly, she liked men who brought her home at dawn.

This entire infatuation was built on nothing. Lisa was a detective, a wife, and a mother. She didn't have time for a destructive fantasy.

Lisa grabbed her travel mug, poured her coffee, and grabbed her keys off the counter. She walked out the front door and didn't look back at Carla's house once. The game was officially over.

——-

Later that day, Lisa pulled her unmarked cruiser into the driveway just as the sun was setting. It had been a grueling Monday. She had spent the entire twelve hour shift burying herself in casework, desperately trying to erase the image of Carla stumbling with a man's arm wrapped around her waist.

She walked through the front door, dropping her keys on the console table and rolling her shoulders to release the tension. She just wanted a quiet evening.

Instead, she found Becky standing at the kitchen sink, aggressively scrubbing a single pan irritated.

"Rough day?" Lisa asked, loosening her tie.

"Not as rough as the show going on next door," Becky snapped, turning off the faucet and wiping her hands on a towel. "It has been going on literally all day, Lisa. Since this morning."

Lisa froze, "What are you talking about?"

"Carla," Becky said, dropping her voice to a whisper even though they were alone. "She brought some guy home at the crack of dawn, and they have been practically having sex on the pool loungers ever since. I had to keep the blinds in the living room closed so Betsy wouldn't see anything when she got home from school."

Lisa’s stomach dropped. She swallowed, her expression remaining entirely blank. "It's her pool, Becky. She can have company over."

"Company is one thing. That was a porno but I guess I shouldn't be surprised, considering what she does for a living. Writing filth all day. It’s inappropriate, Lisa. This is a family neighborhood. We have kids here. She acts like she’s living in some frat house, bringing random men home"

"Mind your own business, Becky," Lisa snapped.

The words agressive. Becky blinked, physically taking a step back, her mouth falling open in shock.

"Excuse me?" Becky yelled her voice trembling with sudden anger.

"I said, mind your own business," Lisa repeated, her voice low. The jealousy she was trying to suppress turned into defensive rage. "She’s a grown woman paying her own mortgage. She isn't breaking any laws. You sitting by the window spying on her all day is arguably creepier than whatever she’s doing in her own backyard."

"I wasn't spying!" Becky’s face flushed a deep, mottled red. "I live here! I have eyes! Why are you defending her? You barely even know her!"

You have no idea what I know about her, Lisa thought bitterly.

"I'm not defending her. I'm just sick of the constant gossip," Lisa lied, rubbing her temples. "I deal with other people's problems all day at work. I don't want to come home and listen to you dissect the neighbor's sex life."

"Oh, so it's my fault now?" Becky crossed her arms, the argument suddenly shifting. "You've been completely checked out since I got back from D.C. You barely looked at me at dinner last night, you've been snappy at me, and now you're taking her side? What is going on with you, Lisa?"

"Nothing is going on," Lisa said tightly.

"Bullshit!" Becky yelled. "You are completely closed off! It's like you're not even in this marriage right now!"

Lisa couldn't do it. She couldn't stand in her perfect kitchen and have this argument while her skin was crawling and her chest felt like it was caving in. Without another word, she turned her back on her wife, walked over to the liquor cabinet, and grabbed the bottle of bourbon. She didn't even bother with a glass.

"Don't you walk away from me!" Becky yelled.

Lisa ignored her. She unlocked the heavy glass back door, stepped out, and slammed the door shut behind her with enough force to shake the windows.

She sat on the patio chair, and unscrewed the cap of the bourbon. She took a long, burning sip straight from the bottle, her hands shaking with adrenaline.

She stared straight ahead into the dark yard, breathing hard, the argument replaying in her head.

A few minutes later, the sound of a car engine starting up broke the quiet. Lisa didn't move her head, but her eyes tracked the movement through the gaps in the wooden fence. The car from this morning backed out of Carla's driveway and drove away.

The guy was gone.

The silence that followed felt incredibly loud. Lisa took another drink of the bourbon, letting the liquor numb her anger.

Then, she heard the soft slide of the glass door next door.

Lisa didn't look over. She kept her eyes fixed firmly on the dark tree line at the edge of her yard.

Carla walked slowly out onto her patio. She was wearing an oversized t-shirt and underwear, and nothing else. She looked exhausted. She ran a hand through her tangled hair and looked toward the fence.

She saw Lisa sitting in the dark, gripping the neck of a bourbon bottle. Staring off into the distance.

Carla stopped. The victory she had felt earlier, proving to herself that she could still go out, still bring someone home, still be entirely unattached completely vanished.

Seeing the detective looking stressed. It concerned her. She had tried to drown out the memory of Lisa's wet tank top and heavy stare by throwing herself at someone else, but it hadn't worked. It hadn't even come close.

Carla took a quiet step toward the fence line, she wanted to check in Lisa.

She just wanted to say hey. She wanted to ask if Lisa was okay.

She was ten feet away from the gate when the glass door on Lisa's patio slid open.

Carla froze, stepping back into the shadows of her own yard.

Becky stepped out. Her anger from the kitchen argument seemed to have ended and replaced by a soft, guilty look. She walked over to where Lisa was sitting, staring into the dark, and knelt down.

"I'm sorry," Becky whispered, Carla could hear them. She reached out, resting her hand gently on Lisa’s knee. "I shouldn't have yelled. You're stressed, and I'm pushing you. I'm sorry."

Lisa didn't say anything for a moment. She just stared at the bourbon bottle in her hand. Then, slowly, she nodded.

Becky stood up and gently took the bottle from Lisa’s hand, setting it on the patio table. She reached down, took both of Lisa's hands in hers, and pulled her up. Lisa stumbled slightly. Becky caught her waist.

Carla stood in the dark. She couldn't look away, even though watching Becky pull Lisa back into the safety of their marriage felt horrible.

Becky guided Lisa toward the open door, stepping inside first. Just as Lisa reached the threshold, she stopped.

She turned her head slowly, looking out into the dark yard. Her eyes found Carla standing in the shadows. They locked eyes. Lisa’s eyes were glassy, unfocused, and completely exhausted. She looked right through Carla, as if the woman standing there was nothing more than a ghost.

Then, Lisa turned her head, stepped over the threshold, and the door slid shut.

Carla let out a shaky breath. She turned around, walked back across her patio, and went inside her own dark, empty house.

She walked straight to her office, opened her laptop. If she couldn't have control in the real world, she was going to take it on the page. She placed her hands on the keys and began to type, pouring every ounce of her frustration, jealousy, and dark, twisted desire into the page.
