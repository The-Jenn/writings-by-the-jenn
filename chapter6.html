Chapter 6: Dinner Guest

Lisa parked her cruiser in the driveway and turned off the engine.

She just sat there for a minute, gripping the steering wheel. She was exhausted. Twelve hours of paperwork, dealing with angry lawyers, and carrying around the stress of Becky. She just wanted to go inside, take a hot shower, and go to sleep.

She stepped out of her car.

She froze.

Sitting on her front porch, sharing a bottle of wine and laughing, were Becky and Carla.

Lisa just stared. Her brain couldn't make sense of it. Just last night, Becky was furious about Carla. She called her inappropriate. She judged her for bringing a guy home. Now, she was pouring Carla wine.

Lisa grabbed her bag and got out of the car.

As she walked up the driveway, Carla turned to look. The laughter stopped.

Lisa was still in her work uniform. Dark gray pants, a blue button down shirt, her gold badge on her belt, and her leather shoulder holster. Carla had never seen Lisa in her actual uniform before.

Carla swallowed hard. She tried to play it cool, crossing her legs and taking a sip of wine, but her hand wasn't entirely steady.

"Hey, honey!" Becky smiled, standing up from her chair.

"Hey," Lisa said. She looked at Carla quick "Carla..."

"Go get changed. I’m gonna go check on dinner," Becky said. She turned to Carla. "I'll be right back."

Lisa opened the front door, let Becky walk in, and shut it behind them.

Once they were in the kitchen, Lisa kept her voice low. "What is going on?" she asked, pointing toward the front porch.

Becky sighed and crossed her arms. "I felt bad, Lisa."

"About what?" Lisa asked.

"The other night," Becky said, looking down. "You were right. I was judging her. I sat there spying on her instead of minding my own business."

Lisa stared at her. Her heart was beating fast.

"So, I saw her getting her mail today," Becky went on. "She looked lonely. So I walked over, said hello, and invited her for dinner. I'm trying to be better, Lisa."

Lisa didn't know what to say. She couldn't yell at Becky for being nice. She couldn't tell her the real reason she didn't want Carla sitting on their porch.

"Okay," Lisa said. Her voice sounded flat. "Let me just go change."

"Take your time," Becky smiled, turning to the stove. "Dinner will be just a little longer."

Lisa walked upstairs and shut her bedroom door. She took a deep breath. This was bad.

She took off her holster and badge and left them on the bed. She unbuttoned her uniform shirt and threw it in the basket.

She went into the bathroom and splashed cold water on her face, staring at herself in the mirror. She just needed to get through dinner.

She put on some jeans and a plain black t-shirt. She walked back downstairs and pushed open the front door and took the seat next to Carla. Becky still inside.

"Dinner will be a few minutes," Lisa said. She sat down in the chair across from her.

Carla didn't say anything at first. She just looked at Lisa. But the way she looked at Lisa said everything.

Carla slowly met her eyes. The look was so heavy, that Lisa felt her face get hot.

"So," Lisa said, clearing her throat. She needed to break the silence. "How's the writing going?"

Carla smiled a little.

"It's going," Carla said. Her voice was quiet. "I'm actually stuck right now."

"Oh?" Lisa asked.

"Yeah. I'm writing a new character," Carla said. "She's a cop. Very strict. Plays by the rules. I'm trying to figure out how to make her lose control."

Lisa’s stomach flipped. She shifted a bit.

"Maybe," Lisa said, keeping her voice steady, "she doesn't want to lose control."

Carla tilted her head.

"Everyone wants to lose control eventually," Carla said softly. "They just need the right person to take it from them."

Before Lisa could even respond, the door opened.

"Dinner's ready!" Becky said.

They sat at the dining table. Becky talked about the neighborhood. Carla smiled and answered her questions.

But it wasn't normal.

Lisa could feel the tension between her and Carla. Lisa had tuned out what was being said between Carla and Becky.

"Lisa is so great," Becky said, smiling at her wife. "She works long hours, but she always takes care of things around the house. I don't know what I'd do without her."

Carla reached for her wine glass. She looked right into Lisa’s eyes.

"That’s wonderful," Carla said.

Lisa swallowed hard. She shifted in her chair. She felt hot. Hearing Becky brag about her while Carla stared at her like that was messing with her head.

Lisa couldn't stop thinking about Carla's hands. She couldn't stop thinking about what she smelled like. Lisa had to cross her legs under the table just to keep still.

When dinner was over, they went out to the back patio to finish their wine.

A few minutes later, the side gate opened. Betsy walked into the yard, she had been at a friends House.

"Hey," Becky smiled. "This is Carla."

"Hi," Betsy said. She looked at Becky. "Mom, I need some help with an assignment, can you please help me?"

"Of course," Becky said. She stood up. "I'll be right back ladies."

Becky and Betsy went inside.

It was totally quiet on the patio.

Lisa tensed up again. They were alone. Again.

"You're quiet," Carla said.

"Just tired," Lisa said.

They started making small talk. Carla asked how long Lisa had lived here. Lisa asked about Carla moving from the city. But the words didn't matter. They weren't listening to what the other person was saying.

They were just staring at each other.

Lisa was breathing a little faster. She kept looking at Carla's lips.

Carla leaned forward. She rested her elbows on her knees. She was sitting much closer now.

Carla looked at Lisa. It wasn't teasing anymore. It was serious. Carla looked at Lisa's mouth, and then down her neck. Her eyes told Lisa exactly what she wanted to do to her.

Lisa stopped breathing.

Carla watched her. Up until tonight, Carla had tried to talk herself out of this. She had tried to tell herself to stay away. Lisa was married. Lisa had a kid. It was messy, and Carla usually avoided messy.

But looking at Lisa right now, seeing the desperate look in her eyes, Carla realized she didn't care anymore.

The moral line didn't matter. She was past trying to do the right thing. She wanted Lisa. Badly. And sitting there in the dark, Carla decided she wasn't going to stop until she got her.

Carla set her wine glass down on the table.

She leaned closer. She didn't touch Lisa, but she was so close Lisa could feel the heat coming off her skin.

"Lisa.. I…." Carla said softly.

"Carla..." Lisa whispered. It was a warning, but it sounded weak.

"Don't pretend, Lisa," Carla said, holding Lisa's gaze.

“You like it when I look at you. And I can't stop looking at you. It's driving me crazy. Seeing you sitting across that table tonight, playing the perfect wife, when all I could think about was what I want to do to you."

Lisa’s eyes went wide. Her heart was beating so hard it hurt. She opened her mouth, but no words came out.

Carla shifted her weight, moving just a fraction of an inch closer.

"I don't care about playing the good neighbor anymore," Carla said. "I'm not going to pretend there isn’t something going on between us. I think you know exactly what I’m talking about."

"I'm married," Lisa whispered.

It was her last line of defense, but she barely believed it herself right now.

Lisa closed her eyes for a second. It was true. Her entire body felt like it was on fire. The want was so heavy it felt like it was crushing her.

Carla reached out. She didn't grab Lisa, but she let one finger trace lightly along the bare skin of Lisa's arm, right where her short sleeve ended. Lisa closed her eyes at the touch.

"You can hide behind your badge and your marriage all you want," Carla whispered, leaving her hand resting just above Lisa's knee. "But you want this just as much as I do."

Right at that moment, the sliding glass opened and Carla pulled her hand back instantly. She leaned back in her chair and picked up her wine glass, her face completely calm.

Becky walked out with a tired smile.

"Sorry about that," Becky said, sitting back down in her chair. "Crisis averted. What did I miss?"

Lisa's hands were shaking so badly she had to put her wine glass down on the table. She stared at Carla.

Carla took a sip of her wine, looked at Becky, and smiled. "Nothing at all. Lisa was just telling me how much she loves her job."
